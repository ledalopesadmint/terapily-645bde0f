# MIN-04 — Respiração Alternada (Nadi Shodhana / Alternate Nostril Breathing)

## Protocol Origin
- **Tradition:** Yoga pranayama (Nadi Shodhana = "channel purification")
- **Clinical adoption:** Integrated into trauma-informed care, yoga therapy programs, VA complementary health
- **Note:** One of the most studied pranayama techniques in peer-reviewed literature

## Clinical Protocol
**Type:** guided_timer | **Duration:** 5 min (5-8 cycles)

### Steps
1. Sit comfortably. Use right hand in Vishnu Mudra (index + middle fingers curled)
2. Close right nostril with thumb. Inhale through left nostril for **4 counts**
3. Close both nostrils. Hold for **2 counts** (optional for beginners)
4. Release right nostril (keep left closed with ring finger). Exhale through right for **4 counts**
5. Inhale through right nostril for **4 counts**
6. Close both. Hold for **2 counts**
7. Release left nostril. Exhale through left for **4 counts**
8. This = 1 complete cycle. Repeat 5-8 times.

**Key instruction:** Always start and end with left nostril. Smooth, quiet breath.

## Evidence Base
1. **Ghiya S, Lee CM (2012).** Influence of alternate nostril breathing on heart rate variability in non-practitioners of yogic breathing. *International Journal of Yoga*, 5(1), 66-69. PMC3276937. — ANB significantly increased parasympathetic HRV markers.
2. **Telles S, et al. (2013).** Blood pressure and heart rate variability during yoga-based alternate nostril breathing practice and breath awareness. *Medical Science Monitor Basic Research*, 19, 270-278. PMC4247229. — ANB reduced systolic BP and increased HRV vs. breath awareness alone.
3. **Sinha AN, et al. (2013).** Assessment of the effects of pranayama on anxiety and depression in hypertensives. *Journal of Education and Health Promotion*, 2, 67. — ANB reduced state anxiety in hypertensive patients.
4. **Sharma VK, et al. (2013).** Effect of fast and slow pranayama on perceived stress and cardiovascular parameters in young health-care students. *International Journal of Yoga*, 6(2), 104-110. — Slow pranayama (including ANB) improved cardiovascular parameters.
