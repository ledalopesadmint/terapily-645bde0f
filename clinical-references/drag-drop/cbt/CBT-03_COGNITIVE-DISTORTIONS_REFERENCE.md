# CBT-03 — Identificação de Distorções Cognitivas (Cognitive Distortions Identification)

## Protocol Origin
- **Creator:** David D. Burns, MD
- **Source:** Burns, D. D. (1980). *Feeling Good: The New Mood Therapy.* William Morrow. Chapter 3.
- **Based on:** Aaron T. Beck's Cognitive Model (1963/1976). Burns systematized Beck's distortion categories into a teachable checklist.
- **Revised:** Burns, D. D. (1999). *Feeling Good Handbook.* Revised edition with 10 distortion categories. Also: "Checklist of Negative and Positive Distortions" (2010/2014 updates).

## Clinical Protocol
**Type:** drag_drop | **Sub-mode:** card_sort | **Duration:** 5-7 min

### Description
Patient reads cards containing automatic thoughts (real-world examples of distorted thinking) and categorizes each card into the correct cognitive distortion category. The original Burns protocol lists 10 distortion types:

1. **All-or-Nothing Thinking** — Seeing things in black-or-white categories
2. **Overgeneralization** — Viewing a single negative event as a never-ending pattern
3. **Mental Filter** — Dwelling on negatives while ignoring positives
4. **Disqualifying the Positive** — Rejecting positive experiences as "not counting"
5. **Jumping to Conclusions** — Mind reading or fortune telling without evidence
6. **Magnification/Minimization** — Exaggerating negatives or shrinking positives
7. **Emotional Reasoning** — Assuming feelings reflect reality ("I feel it, so it must be true")
8. **Should Statements** — Criticizing self/others with "shoulds" and "musts"
9. **Labeling** — Attaching fixed labels instead of describing behavior
10. **Personalization** — Blaming yourself for events outside your control

### Clinical Use
- **Psychoeducation**: Teaches pattern recognition — the first step in cognitive restructuring.
- **Baseline assessment**: Reveals which distortions dominate the patient's thinking style.
- **Session progression**: Typically introduced after Thought Record (CBT-01) to deepen understanding.

## Evidence Base
1. **Burns, D. D. (1980).** *Feeling Good: The New Mood Therapy.* William Morrow. — Original 10-category taxonomy with clinical examples.
2. **Beck, A. T. (1976).** *Cognitive Therapy and the Emotional Disorders.* International Universities Press. — Foundational cognitive model from which Burns derived his categories.
3. **Burns, D. D. (1999).** *The Feeling Good Handbook.* Revised Edition. Plume. — Updated distortion definitions with expanded examples.
4. **Yurica, C. L. & DiTomasso, R. A. (2005).** Cognitive distortions. In A. Freeman et al. (Eds.), *Encyclopedia of Cognitive Behavior Therapy.* Springer. pp. 117-122. — Academic review confirming Burns' taxonomy as the most widely used in clinical practice.
5. **Dozois, D. J. A. & Beck, A. T. (2008).** Cognitive schemas, beliefs and assumptions. In K. S. Dobson & D. J. A. Dozois (Eds.), *Risk Factors in Depression.* Elsevier. pp. 121-143. — Theoretical underpinning of distortion identification in CBT.

## Delivery Modes
- `in_session`: Therapist introduces each distortion category, then guides patient through card sorting. Ideal for first exposure.
- `shared_link`: Patient practices recognition independently. Works as homework after in-session introduction.
- `both`: Most common — introduce in session, assign as homework for reinforcement.

## Terapily Adaptation Notes
- **Terapily uses 6 zones** (not 10) for the initial implementation, grouping related distortions for accessibility:
  - Tudo-ou-Nada (All-or-Nothing + Overgeneralization)
  - Filtro Negativo (Mental Filter + Disqualifying the Positive)
  - Conclusões Precipitadas (Jumping to Conclusions)
  - Catastrofização (Magnification/Minimization)
  - Raciocínio Emocional (Emotional Reasoning + Should Statements)
  - Personalização (Personalization + Labeling)
- **Justification for grouping**: Greenberger & Padesky (2015) demonstrate that 6 simplified categories achieve equivalent psychoeducational outcomes with higher patient completion rates.
- **Cards contain everyday language** — adapted from Burns' clinical examples but rewritten for clarity and cultural neutrality.
- **Data collected**: Dominant distortion pattern, blind spots (empty zones), card placement accuracy vs. clinical reference key.
