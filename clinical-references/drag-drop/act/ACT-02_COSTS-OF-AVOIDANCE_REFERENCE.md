# ACT-02 — Custos da Evitação (Costs of Avoidance)

## Protocol Origin
- **Creator:** Russ Harris
- **Source:** Harris, R. (2008). *The Happiness Trap: How to Stop Struggling and Start Living.* Trumpeter Books. Chapter on Experiential Avoidance. pp. 29-31.
- **Worksheet:** "Costs of Avoidance" + "Avoidance & Suffering Diary" from *ACT Made Simple* worksheets collection.
- **Based on:** Experiential Avoidance construct — Hayes, S. C., Wilson, K. G., Gifford, E. V., Follette, V. M., & Strosahl, K. (1996). *Journal of Consulting and Clinical Psychology*, 64(6), 1152-1168.

## Clinical Protocol
**Type:** drag_drop | **Sub-mode:** card_sort | **Duration:** 6-8 min

### Description
Patient receives cards describing common avoidance behaviors and categorizes them by personal frequency: "I Do This Often," "Sometimes," and "Rarely/Never." The exercise builds awareness of avoidance patterns and their cumulative costs — the cornerstone of ACT's case for psychological flexibility.

### Common Avoidance Behaviors (Harris, 2008)
- Procrastination / putting things off
- Substance use (alcohol, drugs, food)
- Excessive screen time / distraction
- Social withdrawal / isolation
- Overworking / staying busy
- Rumination / overthinking
- Reassurance-seeking
- Avoiding difficult conversations
- Physical avoidance of places/people
- Suppressing emotions / "toughing it out"

### Clinical Use
- **Awareness building**: Patient sees the full pattern of avoidance — not just individual behaviors.
- **Motivation for change**: Seeing accumulated costs motivates willingness (ACT core process).
- **Functional analysis**: Feeds into understanding avoidance as a short-term relief / long-term cost cycle.

## Evidence Base
1. **Harris, R. (2008).** *The Happiness Trap.* Trumpeter Books. — Original costs of avoidance exercise with clinical rationale.
2. **Harris, R. (2009).** *ACT Made Simple.* New Harbinger. — Expanded worksheet versions with clinical scripts.
3. **Hayes, S. C., Wilson, K. G., Gifford, E. V., Follette, V. M., & Strosahl, K. 4. **Chawla, N. & Ostafin, B. (2007).** Experiential avoidance as a functional dimensional approach to psychopathology: An empirical review. *Journal of Clinical Psychology*, 63(9), 871-890. doi:10.1002/jclp.20400 — Meta-review of avoidance as transdiagnostic mechanism.
5. **Kashdan, T. B., Barrios, V., Forsyth, J. P., & Steger, M. F. (2006).** Experiential avoidance as a generalized psychological vulnerability. *Behaviour Research and Therapy*, 44(9), 1301-1320. — Evidence for avoidance as cross-diagnostic risk factor.

## Delivery Modes
- `in_session`: Therapist explores costs of top avoidance behaviors after sorting. "What has this pattern cost you?"
- `shared_link`: Patient sorts and reflects between sessions; reviewed in follow-up.
- `both`: Sort in session, then daily Avoidance & Suffering Diary as homework.

## Terapily Adaptation Notes
- **3 sorting zones**: "Faço Frequentemente," "Às Vezes," "Raramente/Nunca."
- **12 cards** with common avoidance behaviors in everyday language — drawn from Harris' published list.
- **No judgment framing**: Instructions emphasize curiosity, not self-criticism ("Notice patterns, don't judge them").
- **Data collected**: Frequency distribution, dominant avoidance cluster, changes between sessions.
- **Therapist report includes**: Avoidance profile, high-frequency patterns, suggested ACT interventions (willingness exercises, defusion) mapped to specific behaviors.
