# ACT-01 — Values Card Sort (Classificação de Valores)

## Protocol Origin
- **Creator:** Russ Harris (popularizer); Kelly G. Wilson & Amy R. Murrell (original values work in ACT)
- **Source:** Harris, R. (2009). *ACT Made Simple.* New Harbinger. Chapter on Values.
- **Also:** Wilson, K. G. & Murrell, A. R. (2004). Values work in Acceptance and Commitment Therapy. In S. C. Hayes & K. D. Strosahl (Eds.), *A Practical Guide to Acceptance and Commitment Therapy.* Springer. pp. 120-151.
- **Instrument:** Tobias Lundgren's Bull's Eye Values Survey; Russ Harris' Values Worksheet.
- **Based on:** Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT) — Steven C. Hayes (1999)

## Clinical Protocol
**Type:** drag_drop | **Sub-mode:** card_sort | **Duration:** 8-10 min

### Description
Patient receives cards representing life values across 8 domains and sorts them into three priority tiers: "Very Important," "Important," and "Less Important Right Now." The exercise clarifies what matters most and reveals discrepancies between stated values and current actions.

### The 8 Value Domains (Harris/Wilson)
1. **Family** — Relationships with parents, siblings, children
2. **Intimate Relationships** — Partner, romance, connection
3. **Friendship/Social** — Friends, community, belonging
4. **Work/Career** — Professional growth, contribution, purpose
5. **Education/Growth** — Learning, personal development
6. **Recreation/Leisure** — Fun, hobbies, play, creativity
7. **Health/Body** — Physical well-being, self-care
8. **Spirituality/Meaning** — Religion, philosophy, existential purpose

### Clinical Use
- **Values clarification**: Foundation exercise in ACT protocols (typically session 2-4).
- **Motivation enhancement**: Reveals what drives the patient beyond symptom reduction.
- **Committed action planning**: Results feed into behavioral activation and goal-setting.

## Delivery Modes
- `in_session`: Therapist facilitates discussion after sorting — "What surprised you about your ranking?"
- `shared_link`: Patient sorts independently; results reviewed in next session.
- `both`: Sort in session with discussion, repeat at home for deeper reflection.

## Terapily Adaptation Notes
- **3 sorting zones**: "Muito Importante," "Importante," "Menos Importante Agora" — following Harris' three-tier model.
- **8 value cards** with descriptions — one per domain, using accessible language.
- **No "right answers"**: Unlike distortion identification, values sorting has no reference key. All placements are valid.
- **Data collected**: Priority ranking, time spent on each card (hesitation = ambivalence indicator), distribution pattern (all in one zone = possible disengagement).
- **Therapist report includes**: Value hierarchy, potential values-action gaps (flagged for follow-up), comparison across sessions if repeated.
